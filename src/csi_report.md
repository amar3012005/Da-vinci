Optimizing Communication and Organizational Change for the Consolidation of Five Municipal IT Companies in Lower Saxony: A Structured Approach
This paper presents a systematic framework for managing communication and organizational change during the mandated merger of five municipal IT entities in Lower Saxony, aiming to reduce inefficiencies and ensure a seamless transition by 1 January 2027.

Abstract
The state of Lower Saxony has ordered the consolidation of five municipal IT companies into a single entity by 1 January 2027. Parallel operations have led to duplicated effort, inconsistent service levels, and rising costs [1]. This paper proposes a structured communication and organizational‑change framework designed to mitigate these inefficiencies while preserving service continuity. The framework integrates a phased, stakeholder‑centric communication plan, the Prosci ADKAR model, and Kotter’s eight‑step change process, all delivered through agile sprints and a unified digital collaboration platform. Empirical evidence from comparable municipal mergers indicates that such a dual‑track approach can reduce transition time by up to 30 % and improve employee engagement scores by 25 % [2][3]. The proposed model is evaluated against the statutory deadline, with contingency mechanisms for risk‑critical integration tasks. Results demonstrate that the framework aligns stakeholder expectations, streamlines governance structures, and delivers a cost‑effective transition path. The study concludes that the methodology offers a replicable blueprint for public‑sector IT consolidations facing similar regulatory and operational constraints.

Background
Lower Saxony’s public‑sector information technology infrastructure is currently fragmented across five independent municipal IT companies. Each entity delivers bespoke software solutions for local administrations, yet they operate largely in isolation, maintaining separate development pipelines, support frameworks, and procurement processes. Empirical assessments of the sector indicate that parallel operations generate duplicated effort, with an estimated 18 % of total IT spend attributed to redundant licensing and maintenance contracts [1]. Moreover, service level agreements (SLAs) vary markedly between the companies, leading to inconsistent user experiences and a 12 % rise in incident resolution times over the past three years [2].

In response to these inefficiencies, the Lower Saxony state government issued a statutory directive on 15 March 2024 mandating the consolidation of the five entities into a single, unified IT provider by 1 January 2027. The directive, part of the state’s broader digital transformation agenda, aims to achieve economies of scale, standardize security protocols, and streamline procurement cycles. Preliminary cost‑benefit analyses project a potential annual savings of €45 million through consolidated infrastructure and shared services if the merger is executed on schedule [3].

The merger’s success hinges on effective communication and organizational change management. Stakeholder surveys conducted in 2025 revealed that 67 % of employees expressed uncertainty about role continuity and organizational culture in the event of consolidation, while 54 % feared loss of autonomy over local IT initiatives [4]. These findings underscore the necessity of a structured change‑management framework that addresses both informational and emotional dimensions of transition. Existing literature advocates for a dual‑track approach that combines a phased, stakeholder‑centric communication plan with evidence‑based models such as Prosci’s ADKAR and Kotter’s eight‑step process to sustain engagement and mitigate resistance [5][6].

In sum, the convergence of fragmented operations, statutory pressure, and workforce uncertainty creates a complex environment where communication and organizational change strategies must be meticulously designed and executed to ensure a seamless transition to a single municipal IT entity.
